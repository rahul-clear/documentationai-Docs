The 5-corner model is a decentralised digital invoicing framework used across C5-based e-invoicing mandates, where five interconnected parties collaborate to validate, transmit, and monitor electronic invoices. It marks a shift to decentralised digital tax compliance, ensuring secure invoice exchange, real-time validation, and visibility for the tax authority.

## The five corners

- **Corner 1 - Supplier** - creates and issues the structured e-invoice.
- **Corner 2 - Supplier's Access Point (e.g. ClearTax)** - validates, formats, and transmits the invoice in the standard digital format.
- **Corner 3 - Buyer's Access Point** - receives, verifies, and delivers the invoice to the buyer.
- **Corner 4 - Buyer** - accepts and processes the invoice into their accounting/ERP systems.
- **Corner 5 - Tax Authority platform** - monitors transactions for audit and compliance (e.g. the FTA in the UAE).

## How the transaction flows

1. **Invoice generation** - the supplier creates the invoice in the prescribed electronic format.
2. **Validation and transmission** - the supplier's Access Point confirms the structure and sends it via secure channels.
3. **Reception and verification** - the buyer's Access Point receives and validates the document.
4. **Buyer acceptance** - the buyer electronically accepts the invoice.
5. **Real-time reporting** - critical invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority platform simultaneously.

## Key technical features

- **Decentralised architecture** - the network runs through accredited service providers that facilitate data exchange, allowing secure transactions without overloading a single central authority.
- **Continuous Transaction Control (CTC)** - every invoice submitted through the system is authenticated, traceable, and reportable to the tax authority in near real-time.
- **Accredited Service Providers (ASPs)** - only certified ASPs can participate in invoice exchange, ensuring reliability, data integrity, and compliance with the tax authority's technical and security standards.
- **Structured invoice format** - invoices are generated in a structured format, typically XML or JSON, adhering to the country's standardised schema, eliminating ambiguity and supporting seamless system integration.
- **Peppol framework integration** - the model leverages the Peppol interoperability network, maintaining consistency with international invoicing standards and facilitating cross-border digital trade.
- **Two-way acknowledgment mechanism** - both supplier and buyer receive status notifications (submission, acceptance, delivery confirmation), ensuring end-to-end traceability.
- **Data security and encryption** - all transmissions are encrypted and validated, in line with applicable cybersecurity laws and data protection protocols.

## Benefits for businesses

- **Automation and efficiency** - electronic exchange minimises manual entry, duplication, and reconciliation errors.
- **Faster invoice lifecycle** - real-time transmission speeds up validation, approval, and payment.
- **Accuracy of data** - structured digital formats support error-free tax reporting.
- **Reduced compliance costs** - centralised systems reduce administrative overhead and audit risk.
- **Improved cash flow** - real-time invoice status supports better financial planning.
- **Global interoperability** - alignment with global interoperability systems facilitates invoicing and trade across borders.

## Benefits for tax authorities

- **Real-time compliance monitoring** - direct access to transaction data helps identify anomalies and curb tax evasion and fraud.
- **Better policy insight** - aggregated data supports fiscal and economic decision-making.
- **Streamlined VAT administration** - automated data collection simplifies audit trails and reconciliations.
- **Accelerated digital transformation** - builds national infrastructure for future regulatory innovation.
